01 / The business questionLook beyond the sales line

GROWTH NEEDED
TO GIVE BACK.

Atone was adding sales and customers without a corresponding improvement in profit. Operating expenses had increased, and advertising performance was part of the challenge.

StoreHero’s account describes bringing order profitability, product profitability and operating costs into clearer view. That information helped inform product, marketing and cost decisions.

02 / The Collective perspectiveWhat the case brings into focus
The commercial question is what the additional sale leaves behind.
The order

Understand the value of winning it.

Acquisition decisions need a view of the costs attached to the sale. Product costs, discounts, payment fees, fulfilment and returns all matter to that question. More volume is only part of the picture.

The useful conversation is about which customers and orders the business can serve well, what it can afford to invest in acquiring them, and how that judgement changes as the evidence develops.

The product

Connect the offer to the economics.

A popular product and a valuable product are not always the same thing. Looking at the mix gives strategy, merchandising and campaigns a shared reference point.

It also raises practical questions: which products deserve more attention, what is available to fulfil, and whether an offer encourages a purchase that makes sense for both the customer and the business.

The business

Keep the wider costs visible.

A marketing report is one view of performance. It needs to sit beside the costs and operating conditions that affect what the business keeps.

That connection gives a review meeting a useful purpose: deciding what to continue, what to change and what deserves the next investment. The objective is a better decision, with the evidence and responsibility for acting on it clear.

03 / Reading the evidenceSource & attribution

A RESULT.
ITS CONTEXT.

The reported measures are distinct. Profit on new-customer orders is not the same measure as total revenue, repeat-customer sales or operating expenses. Keeping those labels intact matters.

This case provides a useful starting point for a conversation about commercial measurement. It does not establish what another business will achieve. That depends on its own economics, starting position, priorities and execution.
