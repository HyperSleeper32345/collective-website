THE WORK.

David’s relationship with Little Fish began through his work at King Kong and included consulting before and after his time inside the business. This account focuses on his in-house role as Sales & Marketing Manager.

The role brought marketing and sales into the same field of view: the interest generated, the conversations that followed and the information the business needed to understand its growth.

01 / The commercial contextA considered purchase

FOLLOW THE
OPPORTUNITY.

A high-value property-development service asks a customer to make a considered decision. The first enquiry is one moment in a longer journey involving information, trust and a conversation about the right way forward.

David’s work covered the systems, marketing activity and customer handling around that journey. It connected the practical work of generating interest with the decisions and processes inside the business.

02 / The workFive connected workstreams
01 / Visibility

CRM and reporting.

Developing the systems used to manage customer relationships and examine marketing performance. The focus was the information available to understand how opportunities progressed and support the next commercial decision.

02 / Conversion

The offer and the enquiry journey.

Contributing to service and product development alongside landing-page improvements. The business proposition and the next step for the customer needed to work together.

03 / Delivery

Paid activity brought in-house.

Moving paid advertising from external agency management into the business. This brought campaign management closer to the wider sales and marketing work and the decisions being made internally.

04 / Operations

Sales processes and appointment booking.

Using automation to support parts of the sales process and scheduling. This was practical operational work around how a prospective customer moved from an enquiry into a conversation.

05 / Demand

The company’s voice.

Supporting demand-generation activity and the launch and distribution of the company podcast. The work contributed to the business’s wider presence alongside the activity intended to produce an immediate response.

03 / What the experience informsThe Collective perspective

THE DECISION.

Working inside the business gave David a view of both the marketing activity and what happened after an enquiry arrived. That experience informs Collective’s approach to high-value, considered purchases today.

Measurement needs to follow the opportunity far enough to support a useful decision. The work needs clear ownership. Content, acquisition, customer handling and reporting need a shared commercial objective.

For a developer building a lasting business, those connections reach across individual projects. They shape how the company is understood, how interest is handled and what the business learns from each opportunity.
