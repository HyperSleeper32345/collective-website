The shape of an independent growth business

By Collective

An independent growth business starts with a practical question: what does this business need to understand, decide and do to grow well?

In this note

The answer may involve strategy, acquisition, content, conversion, sales processes or the systems that connect them. It may also mean choosing fewer priorities and following them through properly.

That is the model we are building at Collective. It reflects a view about how the work can be organised, with commercial judgement, specialist expertise and operating capability connected around the business.

Start with the decision

Businesses rarely experience their growth problems in neat service categories. An acquisition campaign can reveal a weak offer. A website enquiry can expose an unclear handover. A promising sales pipeline can be difficult to interpret because the stages mean different things to different people.

The first responsibility is to understand the commercial question. What is happening? What evidence supports that view? What would change the next decision?

A useful starting brief makes that concrete:

  • The situation we are trying to understand.
  • The evidence available and what remains uncertain.
  • The decision that needs to be made.
  • The work, owner and measure that follow from it.

That gives the activity a purpose and creates something the people involved can return to when priorities compete.

More capability makes judgement more important

AI has expanded what we can research, produce and connect within Collective. It supports the preparation of material, development of ideas and operation of the work.

That additional capability creates more options. Someone still has to decide which options deserve attention, whether the evidence is reliable and whether the work is appropriate for the business.

Commercial priorities, brand judgement and responsibility for a decision need to remain visible. A client should understand why a piece of work exists and who is accountable for it. The quality of that relationship matters as the available tools become more capable.

Keep direction close to delivery

A strategy gains value through the decisions and work that follow it. Delivery also produces information that should change the strategy.

Keeping those activities connected allows observations from campaigns, customer conversations, content and operations to inform the next move. It makes the relationship between the plan and the work easier to examine.

At Collective, David leads the commercial direction and stays close to execution. Specialist expertise supports delivery where it adds value. HyperCtrl provides operating infrastructure behind the work.

The arrangement is intended to keep information moving and decisions understandable. A finding from the sales conversation should be able to influence the message. A change in the economics should be able to influence the acquisition plan.

Define the fit through the business

Industry knowledge matters. So do the characteristics of the commercial problem.

A repeat-purchase business needs to understand acquisition, contribution and the reasons customers return. A business with a longer, more considered purchase needs to understand trust, progression, follow-up and the eventual decision.

The work differs, but both require a clear view of the customer journey and the economics around it. Our fit is strongest where the decision-maker is involved, the information needed for measurement can be shared, and the business has the willingness and resources to act.

Make the responsibility explicit

A wider capability needs an agreed scope. The relationship still needs priorities, delivery capacity, clear responsibilities and a shared understanding of the result being pursued.

Longer sales cycles also require patience in how evidence is interpreted. Earlier indicators can help guide decisions, while commercial outcomes establish what the work ultimately contributed. Those measures should be defined together, with their limits understood.

Collective is being built around that combination: direct judgement, connected execution and a clear commercial reason for the work. The model will develop as the capability develops. Its value has to remain visible in the decisions it helps a business make and the work that follows.
