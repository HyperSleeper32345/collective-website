01

Collective measures profit per order rather than reporting revenue.

The measure is contribution margin per order. It is revenue less cost of goods, shipping, payment processing, pick and pack, and variable advertising cost, divided by orders.

The data comes from your own systems. Shopify supplies revenue, orders, new customers, product costs and inventory. The advertising platforms supply one figure, which is spend.

Collective counts new customers in Shopify, not in the advertising platform. Those platforms over-count through their own attribution, so Collective treats their conversion data as a cross-check and never as the denominator.

Before Collective pulls a figure, it checks three things in your store.

  • Whether you maintain per-variant product costs and keep them current.
  • Whether you track inventory, including incoming transfers and purchase orders.
  • Whether Collective can reach your payment payout reports.

That check decides which figures Collective reads live and which it derives. Where it can neither read nor derive a figure, it uses a published benchmark and marks every place it does.

Collective never benchmarks cash on hand or accounts payable. Those two come from you.

02

Collective reads your Meta account before it changes anything in it.

Collective reads your Meta advertising account with read-only access. It creates, edits, pauses and deletes nothing.

The read runs at account, campaign, ad set and ad level, day by day, across twelve months, on the account's own attribution setting.

Collective then checks what the platform reports against your store's own order records before it uses any of it.

A recommendation is traced when it names the metric it rests on, the comparison that turned that metric into a finding, and the principle it applies. A recommendation that rests on judgment alone is untraced, and the audit labels it judgment.

The audit runs on data your account already holds, so it needs no new advertising spend. Collective delivers it before it proposes any change to spend.

03

One dashboard holds all twelve figures.

Collective has already built this dashboard and can walk you through it before you commit to anything.

The first figure is the measure this page opens with. Each figure is named here with its definition.

Contribution margin per order

Revenue less cost of goods, shipping, payment processing, pick and pack and variable advertising cost, divided by orders.

Average order value

Revenue divided by the number of orders.

Gross margin

Revenue less cost of goods, as a percentage of revenue.

Customer acquisition cost

Advertising spend divided by the number of first-time customers your store recorded.

Lifetime value

Average order value, multiplied by how often a customer buys, multiplied by gross margin.

Lifetime value to acquisition cost

Lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost.

Payback period

Customer acquisition cost divided by the contribution margin the business makes in a day, which is the monthly figure divided by 30. The dashboard reports it in days.

Inventory turnover

Cost of goods divided by average inventory value. The dashboard reports it as a number of turns.

Days inventory outstanding

Average inventory value divided by cost of goods, multiplied by 365. It is how many days stock is held before it sells.

Weeks of cover

Current inventory divided by weekly sales.

Cash conversion cycle

The days stock is held, plus the days between the sale and the money reaching your bank, less the days your suppliers give you.

Working capital required

Inventory, plus money owed to you, less money you owe.

Every figure reduces to a fixed list of inputs. Each input is pulled from your Shopify store, your advertising accounts or your own finance records.
